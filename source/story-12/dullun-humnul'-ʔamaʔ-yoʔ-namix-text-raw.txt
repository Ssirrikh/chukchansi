Dullun Humnul’, Paʔashin Humnul’, ʔamaʔ yoʔ ʔalajaanaʔ
Mountain Quail, Valley Quail, and Water Dog (=salamander)

Dullun Humnul’, Paʔashin Humnul’, ʔamaʔ yoʔ ʔalajaanaʔ gichahantaʔ ʔilp’aw.
Mountain Quail, Valley Quail and Water Dog got locked in a cave.

Dullun Humnul’ ʔamaʔ yoʔ Paʔashin Humnul’ hidyaʔ hik’iytaʔ woshoy’nohiya
Mountain Quail and Valley Quail both played the flute. 

Dullun Humnul’ doshtoʔ Paʔashin Humnula, Deyga hik’iyga!
Mountain Quail told Valley Quail, “Go first, play!” 

Huyuch’ maʔ naʔash diʔshal bokbokshi sheleelaw? 
“Can you make a hole in the rock?” 

Woshoy’nohiy’ jawwan, chipniʔ neyeh.
Flutes had power like a shaman.

Humnul’ hik’iytaʔ woshoy’nohiya, 
The quail played the flute,

ʔohom’ daʔ haʔan hawtaʔ.
but nothing happened.

Miʔin ʔamaʔ Dullun Humnul’ hik’iytaʔ ʔam woshoy’nohiya.
Then Mountain Quail played his flute. 

ʔohom’ ʔamaʔ naʔash ʔodbol ʔilp’a.
He could not open the cave.

Hach’a miʔin ʔalajaanaʔ hik’yeʔ.
Then Water Dog played. 

ʔohom’ daʔ taʔan hik’yeʔ woshoy’nohiya.
He did not play a flute.

ʔamaʔ woshoy’not ʔam yebeech’ilan.
He whistled with his lips.

Haatimtaʔ, Dololinaʔ, dololinaʔ, dol.
He sang, “Dololina, dololina, dol.” 

Haatimtaʔ ʔamaʔ bonyil’.
He sang twice. 

Boneeyataw k’otiʔ bokbokish ʔodbintaʔ sipin’ ʔamaamin. 
The second time a large hole opened right above them. 

Hidyaʔ ʔaman tishtaʔ.
They all got out.

Wat ʔug ʔamaamin hashaʔwemaʔshataʔ. 
Someone had tried to kill them. 

ʔamaʔ taʔach’.
That's all.

[From MW. The words, he said, were nonsense syllables, not "Indian words."]
